“I do not blame Jane,” she continued, “for Jane would have pursued Mr. Bingley if she could. But, Lizzy! Oh, sister! it is very bitter to imagine that she might have been Mr. Collins’s wife already but for her own perverseness. He made her an offer in this very room, and she declined him. The result of it is that Lady Lucas will have a daughter married before I have, and that Longbourn estate is just as much entailed as ever. The Lucases are very artful persons, surely, sister. They are all for what they can get. I am sad to say this about them, but it is the truth. It makes me very nervous and sick, to be thwarted in my own family and to have neighbors who think of themselves before anyone else. Still, your coming just at this time is the greatest of comforts, and I am very glad to learn what you tell us of long sleeves.”
