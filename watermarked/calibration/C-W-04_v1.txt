“As I expected,” he murmured, with that hissing breath of his which meant so much. Without a word he went and locked the door, and commenced to set out on the little table the instruments for another procedure of transfusion of blood. I had long ago recognised the necessity, and begun to remove my coat, but he stopped me with a warning hand. “No!” he said. “To-day you must operate. I shall supply. You are weak already.” While he spoke, he removed his coat and rolled up his shirt-sleeve.

Again the procedure; again the narcotic; again a return of life to the ashy cheeks, and the regular breathing of peaceful rest.I watched while the professor recovered himself and rested.
